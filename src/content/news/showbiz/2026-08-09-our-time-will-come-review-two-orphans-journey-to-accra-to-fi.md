## ‘Our Time Will Come’ Review: A Heartfelt Journey Through Ghana

In a poignant blend of storytelling and cultural representation, "Our Time Will Come," the feature debut of writer-director Amartei Armar, offers audiences an emotionally charged narrative that resonates deeply with the themes of family, loss, and resilience. Drawing inspiration from the classic tale of "Oliver Twist," this Ghanaian drama reimagines the journey of two orphans in search of their roots against the backdrop of contemporary West Africa.

## A Modern Take on a Classic Tale

The film begins with a road trip that sets the stage for a narrative filled with hope and determination. Unlike the often grim portrayals of orphaned characters in similar stories, Armar's approach is refreshingly compassionate. He steers clear of relentless despair, instead providing a nuanced exploration of the struggles faced by his protagonists. This choice allows viewers to engage with the characters on a deeper level, fostering empathy and understanding.

## The Journey to Accra

As the orphans embark on their quest to find their kin in Accra, the capital city of Ghana, the film transitions from a simple road trip to a more complex examination of the socio-economic challenges that pervade their environment. Armar skillfully captures the vibrancy of Accra while also shedding light on the harsher realities that many face. The juxtaposition of hope and hardship is a recurring theme, reflecting the duality of life in urban Ghana.

## Emotional Depth and Character Development

The strength of "Our Time Will Come" lies in its character development. The orphans, portrayed with authenticity and depth, navigate a world filled with both kindness and cruelty. Armar's careful attention to their emotional journeys allows the audience to witness their growth and resilience. The film avoids the trap of melodrama, instead opting for a more grounded portrayal of its characters' experiences.

## Cinematic Techniques and Atmosphere

Armar's direction is complemented by a strong visual aesthetic that enhances the storytelling. The cinematography captures the essence of Ghana, from the bustling streets of Accra to the serene landscapes that frame the orphans' journey. The atmospheric quality of the film immerses viewers in the setting, making the orphans' struggles and triumphs feel all the more immediate and relatable.

## Conclusion: A Must-Watch for Global Audiences

"Our Time Will Come" is not just a film about orphans; it is a universal story of belonging and the search for identity. It invites audiences to reflect on the importance of family and the enduring spirit of hope. Amartei Armar's debut is a testament to the power of storytelling, offering a fresh perspective on familiar themes while celebrating the richness of Ghanaian culture. This film is a must-watch for those seeking an emotionally resonant experience that transcends geographical boundaries.
