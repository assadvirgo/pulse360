## "Chapa 100" Gains Momentum at Locarno Open Doors

The film "Chapa 100," directed by Ique Langa from Mozambique, is making waves ahead of its anticipated showcase at the Locarno Film Festival's Africa-focused initiative, Open Doors. This project, which recently secured the 2025 Atlas Workshops Development Award, has garnered attention not only for its compelling narrative but also for its growing production team and international backing.

### A Promising Narrative

"Chapa 100" is a significant entry in the landscape of African cinema, reflecting the unique cultural and social narratives of Mozambique. The film's title refers to a common form of public transportation in the country, known as "chapa," which serves as a vital means of mobility for many citizens. Through this lens, Langa aims to explore themes of community, resilience, and the everyday struggles faced by individuals in Mozambique.

### International Collaboration

The film has recently been picked up by Luminalia, an Italy-based sales agency known for its commitment to promoting independent cinema. This partnership is expected to enhance the film's visibility on the international stage, paving the way for potential distribution opportunities beyond Mozambique. Luminalia's involvement underscores the growing interest in African narratives within the global film market.

In addition to Luminalia's support, "Chapa 100" has welcomed Mo Harawe as an associate producer. Harawe is recognized for his work on "The Village Next to Paradise," which was showcased at the prestigious Cannes Film Festival. His expertise and experience are anticipated to further elevate the production quality and narrative depth of "Chapa 100."

### Open Doors Initiative

Locarno's Open Doors initiative is designed to spotlight emerging filmmakers from Africa and other underrepresented regions, providing them with a platform to share their stories with a global audience. The program offers a range of resources, including mentorship, networking opportunities, and potential funding avenues. By featuring "Chapa 100," Open Doors aims to highlight the diverse voices and perspectives that are shaping contemporary African cinema.

### Looking Ahead

As "Chapa 100" prepares for its debut at the Locarno Film Festival, the film industry is keenly observing its progress. The combination of Langa's vision, Luminalia's backing, and Harawe's production expertise positions the film as a potential standout in the festival lineup. With the increasing recognition of African cinema on the world stage, "Chapa 100" could play a pivotal role in furthering the discourse around African narratives in film.

The film's journey from development to premiere is a testament to the resilience and creativity of filmmakers like Ique Langa, who are committed to telling authentic stories that resonate with audiences both locally and globally. As the festival approaches, anticipation builds around what "Chapa 100" will bring to the cinematic landscape, and how it will contribute to the ongoing evolution of African storytelling in film.
