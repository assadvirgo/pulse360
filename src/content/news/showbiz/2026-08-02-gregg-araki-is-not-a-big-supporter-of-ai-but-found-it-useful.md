## Gregg Araki Navigates AI in Hollywood with "I Want Your Sex"

Gregg Araki, a prominent figure in independent cinema, has returned to the director's chair after more than a decade with his latest film, "I Want Your Sex." As he embarks on this new project, Araki finds himself navigating a Hollywood landscape that has been significantly transformed by advancements in technology, particularly artificial intelligence (AI).

In a recent discussion with Deadline, Araki shared his thoughts on the role of AI in filmmaking. While he expressed reservations about the broader implications of AI in the creative industry, he acknowledged the practical applications it offered during the editing process of his new erotic comedy thriller. "I'm not a big supporter of AI," Araki stated, emphasizing his concerns about the technology's potential to undermine the artistry of filmmaking. However, he also noted that the tools available today can be remarkably effective, describing the experience as "literally insane."

## The Intersection of Art and Technology

Araki's mixed feelings about AI reflect a broader debate within the film industry. Many filmmakers are grappling with the implications of AI on creativity, originality, and the future of storytelling. While some view AI as a threat to the traditional craft of filmmaking, others see it as a valuable tool that can enhance the creative process.

In the case of "I Want Your Sex," Araki utilized AI-driven tools to streamline the editing process, allowing him to focus on the film's narrative and thematic elements. This approach has sparked conversations about the potential for AI to assist rather than replace human creativity in the arts. As filmmakers like Araki experiment with these technologies, they contribute to an evolving dialogue about the role of AI in creative industries.

"I Want Your Sex," which is now playing in theaters, marks Araki's return to filmmaking after a significant hiatus.
