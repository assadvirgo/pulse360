## ‘Objet a’ Explores Desire and Control in a Digital Age

In an era increasingly dominated by technology and digital interactions, Ann Oren's latest film, ‘Objet a,’ delves into the complexities of human desire and control. Premiering at the Locarno Film Festival, the film features performances by Aenne Schwarz and Louis Hofmann, who portray hand surgeons grappling with their own irresistible fixations.

### A Narrative of Desire and Technology

‘Objet a’ is set against a backdrop that resonates with contemporary concerns, including the impacts of war, climate change, and what some have termed "tech fascism." The film's title, which references Jacques Lacan's concept of the "objet petit a," symbolizes the unattainable object of desire, a theme that is intricately woven into the narrative.

As the characters navigate their professional and personal lives, the film raises questions about the nature of intimacy in a world where physical touch is often replaced by interactions through flat screens. The juxtaposition of their surgical precision with their emotional vulnerabilities serves as a poignant commentary on modern relationships.

### Themes of Control and Vulnerability

In interviews, both Schwarz and Hofmann emphasized the film's exploration of control—both in the operating room and in personal relationships. The characters' expertise as surgeons contrasts sharply with their emotional struggles, highlighting how the pursuit of control can lead to a sense of disconnection. This duality reflects a broader societal trend where technology often mediates human interactions, sometimes at the expense of genuine connection.

The film's narrative invites viewers to reflect on their own relationships and the ways in which technology influences their desires and connections. As Schwarz noted, "We touch flat screens more than we touch each other," a statement that encapsulates the film's central theme.

### A Reflection of Contemporary Concerns

Oren's film does not shy away from addressing the pressing issues of our time. The backdrop of war and climate catastrophe serves as a stark reminder of the fragility of human existence and the urgency of authentic connections. In a world where external crises often overshadow personal relationships, ‘Objet a’ encourages viewers to confront their own desires and the ways in which they seek fulfillment.

The film's exploration of "tech fascism" also resonates with current debates about the role of technology in our lives. As society grapples with the implications of surveillance, data privacy, and the commodification of personal interactions, ‘Objet a’ serves as a timely reflection on the balance between technological advancement and the preservation of human intimacy.

### Conclusion

‘Objet a’ stands as a significant contribution to contemporary cinema, blending psychological depth with pressing social commentary. Through the lens of its characters, the film invites audiences to examine their own desires and the impact of technology on their relationships. As it continues to garner attention at film festivals, it promises to spark discussions about the nature of intimacy in an increasingly digital world.
