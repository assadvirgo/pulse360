## Historic Alex Theatre Screens 'The Odyssey' in 70MM After 35 Years

In a remarkable turn of events, the historic Alex Theatre in Los Angeles has re-emerged as a venue for first-run films, screening the critically acclaimed 'The Odyssey' in 70MM format. This marks the first time in 35 years that the theater has hosted a new movie, with its last screening of a first-run film occurring in 1991 with the blockbuster 'Terminator 2: Judgment Day.'

## A Glimpse into the Past

The Alex Theatre, which opened its doors in 1925, has long been a significant landmark in the world of cinema. Known for its stunning architecture and rich history, the venue has been a favorite among film enthusiasts and historians alike. However, as the years progressed, the theater transitioned away from screening new films, focusing instead on hosting special events and classic film showings.

The screening of 'The Odyssey' represents a significant revival for the venue, bringing new life to a space that has been largely dormant in terms of first-run films. This event not only highlights the enduring appeal of the theater but also underscores the growing interest in classic film formats, such as 70MM, which offer a unique and immersive viewing experience.

## The Significance of 70MM

The 70MM format is renowned for its high resolution and superior image quality, making it a preferred choice for filmmakers seeking to create visually stunning works. 'The Odyssey,' which has garnered attention for its artistic merit and innovative storytelling, is particularly well-suited for this format. The decision to screen the film in 70MM at the Alex Theatre reflects a broader trend in the film industry, where there is a renewed appreciation for traditional cinematic experiences.

Film critics and audiences alike have praised 'The Odyssey' for its ambitious narrative and visual artistry. The film's release in such a historic venue adds an extra layer of significance, as it connects contemporary cinema with the rich legacy of film history.
