## Ava DuVernay's '14th' Explores Constitutional Rights Amid Contemporary Challenges

Ava DuVernay's latest documentary, "14th," premiered as the closing-night film at the New York Film Festival, drawing attention for its impassioned examination of the United States Constitution and the ongoing struggles to uphold civil rights. The film opens with a poignant montage of various American presidents, including Franklin D. Roosevelt, John F. Kennedy, and Ronald Reagan, each taking the oath to "preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution of the United States." This powerful introduction sets the stage for a critical exploration of the Constitution's relevance in today's political climate.

## A Timely Reflection on Rights and Responsibilities

"14th" delves into the historical context of the 14th Amendment, which addresses citizenship rights and equal protection under the law. DuVernay's film argues that the principles enshrined in this amendment are increasingly under threat, particularly in light of recent political developments. The documentary highlights the attempts to rescind rights that many Americans have fought hard to secure, drawing parallels between past and present challenges.

The film's narrative is both educational and urgent, as it seeks to remind viewers of the fundamental values that the Constitution represents. DuVernay employs a mix of archival footage, expert interviews, and contemporary examples to illustrate the ongoing struggle for civil rights in the United States. The documentary serves as a call to action, urging citizens to remain vigilant in the face of potential erosions of their rights.

## Engaging with Contemporary Issues

In "14th," DuVernay does not shy away from addressing the current political landscape, particularly the actions and rhetoric of former President Donald Trump and his administration. The film critiques the ways in which political discourse has shifted, often undermining the very tenets of democracy and equality that the Constitution upholds. By connecting historical injustices with modern-day challenges, the documentary fosters a deeper understanding of the importance of constitutional protections.

The film also features voices from activists and scholars who provide insight into the ongoing fight for equality and justice. Their perspectives enrich the narrative, emphasizing that the struggle for civil rights is far from over. DuVernay's ability to weave personal stories with broader historical themes creates a compelling argument for the necessity of civic engagement and awareness.

## Conclusion: A Call to Action

"14th" is more than just a documentary; it is a clarion call for Americans to reflect on their rights and responsibilities as citizens. DuVernay's work encourages viewers to engage with the Constitution not merely as a historical document but as a living framework that requires active participation and defense. As the film concludes, it leaves audiences with a sense of urgency and empowerment, reminding them that the fight for justice and equality is ongoing.

As the nation grapples with complex issues surrounding civil rights, "14th" stands as a timely reminder of the importance of safeguarding the freedoms that define American democracy. Through her passionate storytelling, DuVernay invites viewers to consider their role in preserving the ideals that the Constitution embodies, making it a significant contribution to contemporary discourse on civil rights and constitutional law.
