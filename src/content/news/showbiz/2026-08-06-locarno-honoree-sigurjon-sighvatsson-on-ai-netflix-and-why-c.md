## Sigurjón Sighvatsson Discusses the Future of Film and Festivals Ahead of Locarno Honor

As the Locarno Film Festival prepares to honor Sigurjón Sighvatsson with the prestigious Raimondo Rezzonico Award, the acclaimed producer known for his work on films such as "Wild at Heart" and "Basquiat" shared his insights on the evolving landscape of cinema, the impact of artificial intelligence, and the future of film festivals in an era dominated by streaming giants like Netflix.

### The Impact of AI on Filmmaking

In recent discussions, Sighvatsson has expressed a cautious optimism regarding the integration of artificial intelligence into the film industry. He acknowledges the potential benefits AI can bring to various aspects of filmmaking, from scriptwriting to post-production. However, he also raises concerns about the creative implications of relying too heavily on technology. "AI can assist in the creative process, but it should never replace the human touch that is essential in storytelling," he stated. Sighvatsson believes that while AI tools can enhance efficiency, the heart of cinema lies in the unique perspectives and emotional depth that human creators bring to their work.

### Streaming Services and the Changing Landscape

Sighvatsson's remarks come at a time when streaming platforms like Netflix are reshaping the way audiences consume films. He acknowledges the significant role these platforms play in providing access to a wider range of films, particularly for independent creators who may struggle to find a traditional theatrical release. However, he also cautions against the potential pitfalls of this shift. "The mega cinema era, dominated by a few major players, may overshadow smaller, more intimate films that deserve attention," he noted. Sighvatsson argues that while streaming services offer convenience, they also risk diluting the cinematic experience that festivals and theaters provide.

### The Future of Film Festivals

Reflecting on the future of film festivals, Sighvatsson is optimistic about the resilience of smaller festivals in the face of growing competition from larger events. He believes that festivals like Locarno, which prioritize artistic expression and diverse storytelling, will continue to thrive as audiences seek authentic cinematic experiences. "Smaller festivals have a unique ability to foster community and celebrate independent voices," he remarked. In contrast, he critiques larger festivals, such as Cannes, suggesting that they have lost their way in recent years. "Cannes doesn’t know what it is anymore. It has become more about spectacle than substance," he stated, emphasizing the need for festivals to remain true to their artistic roots.

### Conclusion

As Sigurjón Sighvatsson prepares to receive the Raimondo Rezzonico Award at the Locarno Film Festival, his reflections on the intersection of technology, streaming, and festival culture highlight the ongoing evolution of the film industry. His insights serve as a reminder of the importance of preserving the artistry and emotional resonance that define cinema, even in a rapidly changing landscape. As filmmakers and audiences navigate these shifts, Sighvatsson's perspective underscores the enduring value of storytelling that connects us on a human level.
