## Nick Curwin Announces Retirement from Indie Incubator Matisse

Nick Curwin, a prominent figure in the British television production landscape, has announced his retirement from Matisse, the indie incubator he co-founded. Curwin's departure marks the end of an era for the company, which has played a significant role in nurturing independent production houses in the UK.

### A Legacy in Production

Curwin is best known for founding two notable British production companies: Dragonfly and The Garden. Both firms have made substantial contributions to the television industry, producing a range of acclaimed programming. His vision and leadership have helped shape the landscape of British television, particularly in the realm of independent production.

Under Curwin's guidance, Matisse has become a vital support system for emerging production companies. The incubator has backed several noteworthy projects, including Dan Grabiner's Orchard Studios and James Longman's Jolly Octopus, the latter of which is associated with the British version of the iconic "Saturday Night Live." Matisse has been instrumental in providing resources and mentorship to indie producers, fostering creativity and innovation within the industry.

### Transition of Leadership

With Curwin's retirement, the operational responsibilities of Matisse will transition solely to co-founder Scarlett Ewens. Ewens has been a key player in the incubator's success and is expected to continue Curwin's legacy while steering the organization in new directions. Her leadership will likely focus on expanding Matisse's influence and support for indie productions, ensuring that the incubator remains a relevant and essential part of the UK's creative ecosystem.

### Industry Reactions

The announcement of Curwin's retirement has elicited a range of responses from industry peers and collaborators. Many have expressed gratitude for his contributions to the field and acknowledged the impact of his work on the careers of numerous producers and creatives. His departure is seen as a significant moment in the industry, prompting reflections on the evolution of independent production in the UK.

### Looking Ahead

As Matisse enters this new chapter under Ewens' leadership, the future of independent production in the UK remains promising. The incubator's commitment to nurturing talent and fostering innovative projects will be crucial in maintaining a vibrant and diverse television landscape. Curwin's retirement may mark the end of his direct involvement, but his influence will undoubtedly continue to resonate within the industry he helped to shape.

In conclusion, Nick Curwin's retirement from Matisse signifies a pivotal moment for both the incubator and the broader British television production community. His legacy of fostering independent creativity will be carried forward by Ewens and the many producers who have benefited from Matisse's support. As the industry evolves, the foundations laid by Curwin will continue to inspire future generations of storytellers.
