## Iranian Filmmaker Majid Majidi Denounces U.S. War at Busan Film Festival

Renowned Iranian filmmaker Majid Majidi, known for his poignant storytelling and visual artistry, has made headlines at the Busan International Film Festival by vocally criticizing the impact of war on ordinary Iranians. His remarks come as his latest film, "Midday Meal," has emerged as a standout feature at the prestigious festival.

### A Significant Presence at Busan

Majidi, who is an Oscar-nominated director, initially contemplated skipping the festival due to the ongoing geopolitical tensions and the toll of war on his homeland. However, he ultimately decided to attend, motivated by a desire to raise awareness about the struggles faced by the Iranian people in the context of international conflicts, particularly those involving the United States.

In his statements, Majidi emphasized that the repercussions of war extend far beyond the battlefield. He described the war as a "midday meal," suggesting that it is a recurring and normalized aspect of life for many Iranians, who are forced to navigate the complexities and hardships that arise from ongoing hostilities. This metaphor underscores the pervasive nature of conflict in the lives of ordinary citizens, who often bear the brunt of decisions made far from their homes.

### "Midday Meal": A Reflection of Reality

"Midday Meal," which has garnered attention for its compelling narrative and emotional depth, reflects the everyday realities faced by those living in a war-torn society. The film delves into themes of resilience, hope, and the human spirit, resonating with audiences who are familiar with the struggles of life under the shadow of conflict.

Majidi's work has long been characterized by its focus on social issues and the human condition. His films often highlight the challenges faced by marginalized communities, and "Midday Meal" is no exception. The film's reception at Busan has reignited discussions about the role of cinema in addressing pressing social issues and the power of storytelling to foster empathy and understanding.

### The Role of Cinema in Advocacy

During his time at the festival, Majidi also spoke about the broader role of cinema as a platform for advocacy and change. He believes that filmmakers have a responsibility to shine a light on the injustices faced by their communities and to use their art to promote dialogue and awareness. By sharing the stories of ordinary Iranians, Majidi hopes to challenge stereotypes and foster a greater understanding of the complexities of life in Iran.

The Busan International Film Festival, which celebrates diverse voices in cinema, provides a crucial space for filmmakers like Majidi to share their perspectives and connect with global audiences. As the festival continues to showcase a wide array of international films, the presence of influential figures like Majidi highlights the importance of storytelling in addressing global issues.

### Conclusion

Majid Majidi's participation in the Busan International Film Festival serves as a powerful reminder of the intersection between art and activism. Through his film "Midday Meal" and his outspoken criticism of war, Majidi underscores the urgent need for empathy and understanding in a world often divided by conflict. As audiences engage with his work, they are invited to reflect on the human cost of war and the resilience of those who endure its consequences.
