## AI-Native Studios and Traditional Studios: A Complementary Future in Entertainment

At the recent Busan International Film Festival, VFX veteran Hong Jae Cheol shared insights on the evolving landscape of the entertainment industry, particularly the interplay between AI-native studios and traditional production houses. As a producer-director at Viscode and a former member of the renowned Industrial Light & Magic team, Hong's perspective is informed by years of experience in visual effects and film production.

### The Rise of AI-Native Studios

Hong highlighted the capabilities of small, AI-native studios that leverage automated pipelines to create original intellectual property (IP). These studios, often more agile and innovative, can rapidly develop new content without the extensive resources typically associated with larger, traditional studios. The use of artificial intelligence in content creation allows these companies to streamline processes, reduce costs, and experiment with new ideas that may not fit within the conventional frameworks of established studios.

The ability of AI-native studios to generate original IP is a significant shift in the entertainment landscape. With the rapid advancement of technology, these studios can create compelling narratives and characters that resonate with audiences, thus carving out their own niche in a competitive market.

### Traditional Studios: Strengths and Advantages

Despite the rise of AI-native studios, Hong emphasized that traditional studios still hold significant advantages. Established franchises, production infrastructure, and a wealth of talent are critical assets that allow these studios to maintain their edge. The experience and expertise of seasoned professionals in traditional studios contribute to the quality and depth of the content produced, often resulting in blockbuster films and series that dominate the market.

Moreover, traditional studios possess robust distribution networks that are essential for reaching a global audience. This capability is particularly important in an industry where visibility and accessibility can significantly impact a project’s success.

### A Complementary Relationship

Rather than viewing AI-native studios and traditional studios as direct competitors, Hong advocates for a more collaborative approach. He believes that both types of studios can coexist and complement each other. While AI-native studios push the boundaries of creativity and innovation, traditional studios can provide the stability and experience necessary for large-scale productions.

This synergy could lead to a more diverse array of content, appealing to a broader audience. As AI-native studios continue to innovate, they may inspire traditional studios to adapt and incorporate new technologies into their workflows, ultimately benefiting the entire industry.

### Conclusion

The insights shared by Hong Jae Cheol at the Busan International Film Festival underscore a pivotal moment in the entertainment industry. As AI-native studios emerge with fresh ideas and methodologies, traditional studios must leverage their strengths to remain relevant. The future of entertainment may well depend on the collaboration between these two distinct yet complementary entities, paving the way for a richer and more varied media landscape.
