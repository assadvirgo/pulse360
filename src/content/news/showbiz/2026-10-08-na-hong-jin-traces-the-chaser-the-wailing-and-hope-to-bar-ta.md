## Na Hong-Jin Explores Themes of War and Culture at Busan Masterclass

Renowned South Korean filmmaker Na Hong-Jin recently captivated audiences at the Busan International Film Festival with a masterclass that delved into the thematic undercurrents of his acclaimed films, including "The Chaser," "The Wailing," and his latest work, "Hope." The session provided insights into how personal convictions and societal fears shape cinematic narratives.

### The Genesis of "Hope"

During the masterclass, Na revealed that "Hope," released in 2021, was conceived during a period of heightened tension on the Korean peninsula. In 2017, as geopolitical tensions escalated, news of U.S. military activities in the region dominated headlines. Na recounted his feelings of anxiety and foreboding during this time, stating, "I had a conviction that war was about to break out." This atmosphere of uncertainty and fear served as a catalyst for the film, which explores the fragility of human life and the impact of external conflicts on personal stories.

### Cultural Influences and Storytelling

Na's works often reflect a deep engagement with Korean culture and societal issues. He emphasized the importance of bar talk and the role of shamans in Korean society as significant influences on his storytelling. "Bar talk," a colloquial term for informal conversations in social settings, serves as a backdrop for many of his narratives, allowing characters to express their fears, hopes, and dreams in a relatable manner. This element adds a layer of authenticity to his films, grounding them in the lived experiences of ordinary people.

The incorporation of shamans into his stories further enriches the cultural tapestry of his films. Na explained that shamans represent a connection to the spiritual world, often acting as intermediaries between the living and the dead. This theme is particularly evident in "The Wailing," where supernatural elements intertwine with the human experience, reflecting the complexities of life in a rapidly changing society.

### Reflections on War and Humanity

Throughout the masterclass, Na articulated a profound concern for the human condition amidst the backdrop of war. He believes that fear of conflict can overshadow the beauty of life, leading to a pervasive sense of despair. In "Hope," he aims to counter this narrative by highlighting resilience and the capacity for hope even in the darkest times. "I want to show that despite the chaos around us, there is still a light that can guide us through," he remarked.

Na's films have garnered international acclaim for their ability to blend suspense with deep emotional resonance. His unique storytelling approach not only entertains but also provokes thought about the broader implications of societal issues, particularly in the context of Korean history and culture.

### Conclusion

As the Busan International Film Festival continues to showcase diverse voices in cinema, Na Hong-Jin's masterclass serves as a reminder of the power of film to reflect and respond to the complexities of human experience. Through his exploration of themes such as war, culture, and hope, Na invites audiences to engage with the narratives that shape our understanding of the world and our place within it. His work stands as a testament to the enduring impact of storytelling in times of uncertainty.
