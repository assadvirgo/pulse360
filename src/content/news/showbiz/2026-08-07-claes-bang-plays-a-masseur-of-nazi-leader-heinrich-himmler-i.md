## Claes Bang Portrays Heinrich Himmler's Masseur in "I Is Another"

Claes Bang, the acclaimed Danish actor known for his diverse roles in film and television, takes on a challenging character in his latest project, "I Is Another." Premiering at the Locarno Film Festival, the film is directed by Felix Randau and explores the complex life of Felix Kersten, who served as the personal physical therapist to Heinrich Himmler, one of the most notorious figures of the Nazi regime.

## A Complicated Historical Narrative

"I Is Another" delves into the true story of Felix Kersten, who claimed to have used his position to save numerous lives during World War II. Kersten's relationship with Himmler was fraught with moral ambiguity; while he had access to one of the architects of the Holocaust, he also purportedly leveraged that access to protect individuals from persecution. This duality presents a rich narrative landscape for Bang, who is tasked with portraying a character that straddles the line between complicity and altruism.

The film raises questions about the ethical implications of collaboration and the extent to which one can influence a figure like Himmler. Critics have noted that while Kersten's claims have been met with skepticism, they nonetheless provide a compelling backdrop for the exploration of human agency in times of extreme moral crisis.

## Bang's Diverse Range of Characters

In discussing his role in "I Is Another," Bang reflected on the complexity of portraying such a historically significant figure. He noted that playing Himmler's masseur requires a nuanced understanding of the character's motivations and the historical context in which he operated. This complexity is a hallmark of Bang's career, as he has consistently chosen roles that challenge both himself and the audience.

Interestingly, Bang also mentioned his character John Paul in the Apple TV+ series "Bad Sisters," describing him as one of his "worst characters." This self-reflection highlights Bang's versatility as an actor, capable of embodying both morally ambiguous figures and outright antagonists. The contrast between his roles in "I Is Another" and "Bad Sisters" underscores his ability to navigate a wide spectrum of human experience, from the deeply flawed to the tragically heroic.

## The Reception and Impact of "I Is Another"

As "I Is Another" makes its debut at the Locarno Film Festival, early reactions suggest that the film is poised to spark significant discussion about its themes and historical implications. Critics have praised Randau's direction and Bang's performance, noting that the film's exploration of morality in the face of tyranny resonates deeply in today's world. 

The film not only seeks to entertain but also to provoke thought about the complexities of human behavior in extreme circumstances. As audiences engage with Kersten's story, they are invited to reflect on the moral choices faced by individuals during one of history's darkest periods.

## Conclusion

Claes Bang's portrayal of Felix Kersten in "I Is Another" adds another layer to his already impressive body of work. As he navigates the moral intricacies of his character's relationship with Heinrich Himmler, Bang continues to challenge himself and his audience, reinforcing his status as one of the most compelling actors of his generation. The film's exploration of historical truth and ethical dilemmas promises to leave a lasting impact on viewers and critics alike.
