## Al-Sharaa Commends U.S. Decision to Remove Syria from Terrorism List

In a significant political development, Syrian Deputy Foreign Minister Faisal Al-Sharaa has expressed his approval of the recent decision by former U.S. President Donald Trump to remove Syria from the list of state sponsors of terrorism. This move, which has drawn mixed reactions internationally, marks a pivotal shift in the diplomatic landscape surrounding Syria.

### Context of the Decision

The United States had designated Syria as a state sponsor of terrorism since 1979, primarily due to its support for various militant groups in the region. This designation has had profound implications for Syria's international relations and economic conditions, contributing to its isolation and the ongoing humanitarian crisis exacerbated by years of conflict.

Trump's administration, however, indicated a willingness to reassess this classification as part of a broader strategy to engage with the Syrian government and potentially facilitate a resolution to the protracted civil war that has plagued the nation since 2011. The decision to remove Syria from the terrorism list was seen by some as an attempt to open diplomatic channels and provide a framework for future negotiations.

### Al-Sharaa's Response

Al-Sharaa's comments reflect a sense of optimism within the Syrian government regarding its international standing. He stated that the removal from the terrorism list is a recognition of Syria's efforts to combat terrorism on its soil and a validation of its sovereignty. The Deputy Foreign Minister emphasized that this decision could pave the way for improved relations with other nations and a potential easing of sanctions that have severely impacted the Syrian economy.

"This decision is a step in the right direction and acknowledges the sacrifices made by the Syrian people in the fight against terrorism," Al-Sharaa remarked during a press conference. He called for a reassessment of other punitive measures against Syria, suggesting that such actions have only served to hinder humanitarian efforts and exacerbate the suffering of ordinary Syrians.

### International Reactions

The decision has not been without controversy. Critics argue that removing Syria from the terrorism list could undermine efforts to hold the Assad regime accountable for its actions during the civil war, including allegations of war crimes and the use of chemical weapons. Human rights organizations have expressed concern that this move could embolden the Syrian government and hinder progress towards a peaceful resolution.

Conversely, some analysts suggest that engaging with Syria could lead to a more stable Middle East by fostering dialogue and cooperation against common threats, such as extremist groups operating in the region. The complexities of Middle Eastern geopolitics mean that any shift in U.S. policy is likely to have far-reaching consequences.

### Conclusion

As the international community grapples with the implications of the U.S. decision, the situation in Syria remains fluid. Al-Sharaa's praise for the removal from the terrorism list underscores the Syrian government's hope for a new chapter in its foreign relations. However, the path forward will require careful navigation of both domestic and international challenges, as the legacy of the civil war continues to shape the future of Syria and its place in the global arena.
