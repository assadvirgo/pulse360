## What Will It Take to Stop the Conflict in Sudan?

The ongoing conflict in Sudan has escalated dramatically over recent months, drawing international attention and concern. As the Sudanese army claims to have retaken a crucial highway linking the capital city of Khartoum to El-Obeid, the question arises: what will it take to bring an end to this protracted conflict?

### Background of the Conflict

The roots of the conflict in Sudan can be traced back to a complex interplay of political, ethnic, and economic factors. Following the ousting of longtime leader Omar al-Bashir in 2019, the country has struggled to establish a stable governance structure. The power vacuum created by his removal has led to increased tensions among various factions, particularly between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

The situation deteriorated further in April 2023 when clashes erupted between these two groups, leading to widespread violence and a humanitarian crisis affecting millions of civilians. The conflict has resulted in thousands of deaths and displaced countless others, compounding the existing challenges faced by Sudan’s population.

### Recent Developments

In a recent military operation, the Sudanese army announced that it had regained control of a key highway connecting Khartoum to El-Obeid. This highway is vital for the movement of goods and people, and its control is seen as a strategic advantage in the ongoing conflict. While this development may provide a temporary boost to the army's position, it raises concerns about the potential for further escalation and violence.

### The Path to Peace

Efforts to broker peace in Sudan have been complicated by a lack of trust between the conflicting parties and the absence of a unified international response. To effectively address the conflict, several key steps are necessary:

1. **Inclusive Dialogue**: A genuine and inclusive dialogue involving all stakeholders, including civil society groups and marginalized communities, is essential. This dialogue must aim to address the underlying grievances that have fueled the conflict.

2. **International Mediation**: The involvement of neutral international mediators can help facilitate discussions and build trust among the conflicting parties. Organizations such as the African Union and the United Nations could play a pivotal role in this process.

3. **Humanitarian Assistance**: Immediate humanitarian aid is crucial to alleviate the suffering of civilians affected by the conflict. Ensuring safe access for humanitarian organizations can help address urgent needs, including food, medical care, and shelter.

4. **Security Sector Reform**: A long-term solution will require comprehensive security sector reform to address the issues of militarization and the role of armed groups in Sudan. This reform should focus on professionalizing the armed forces and integrating various factions into a cohesive national security framework.

5. **International Support**: The international community must remain engaged and committed to supporting peace efforts in Sudan. This includes providing diplomatic pressure on conflicting parties to cease hostilities and adhere to ceasefire agreements.

### Conclusion

The conflict in Sudan is a complex and multifaceted issue that requires a concerted effort from both national and international actors to resolve. While the recent military developments may shift the dynamics on the ground, they do not address the fundamental issues at the heart of the conflict. A sustainable peace in Sudan will only be achievable through inclusive dialogue, international mediation, and a commitment to addressing the underlying causes of violence. The road ahead is fraught with challenges, but the potential for a peaceful resolution remains, contingent upon the willingness of all parties to engage in meaningful dialogue and compromise.
